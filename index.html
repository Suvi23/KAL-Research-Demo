Our Approach

How we turn uncertainty into a plan

A consistent four-step process for rare conditions where data is scarce and every decision matters.

1
Make uncertainty visible

We map what is known, what is contested, and where the real gaps are.

2
Map the full system

Who is publishing, who holds IP, who is working in adjacent space — the whole ecosystem.

3
Find the leverage points

Given your resources, where will effort have the highest return? We build a prioritized roadmap.

4
Collaborate and move

Written artifacts you can share — one-pagers, briefs, memos — and warm introductions to the right researchers.

The R69 Initiative

ICD-10 code R69 — "Illness, unspecified" — is applied to hundreds of thousands of patients who have real, complex conditions but no confirmed diagnosis. We argue that diagnostic uncertainty is a public health problem that deserves its own research agenda.

We convene clinicians, researchers, patient advocates, and health systems to make the case that the undiagnosed population deserves to be counted, studied, and served.

About

The stool has three legs for a reason

The origin

My grandfather kept a three-legged stool in his workshop. He used it to teach a lesson he believed in completely: a stool cannot stand on one or two legs. It needs all three. Knowledge alone is not enough. Action without love is hollow. Love without knowledge leads nowhere.

Those three legs became the name of this practice — and its operating philosophy.

The work

KAL Research Initiatives was founded in Houston in 2018. I work at the intersection of scientific literature and lived experience — translating what researchers are publishing into language and strategy that families, foundations, and companies can actually use.

My background is in biochemistry and physical chemistry. Before founding KAL, I worked as an industry chemist and published in computational chemistry. When I read a paper with a family, I know what the methods actually mean, what the limitations are, and what the authors are not saying.

The community

Current and past collaborative work includes: the SMOC1 Foundation, Wiedemann-Steiner Syndrome Foundation, FAM177A1 Foundation, E.WE, Activate Action, and Melanin Children Matter. Each collaboration has shaped how I think about what families need and what the science can provide.

What this is not

KAL Research does not provide medical advice, diagnoses, or treatment recommendations. We help you understand the science — what the published literature says and what it doesn't — so you can have better conversations with your medical team.
